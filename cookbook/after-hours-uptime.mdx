Outside work hours nobody on your team calls the agent, so a broken agent can go unnoticed until Monday. An uptime monitor calls your agent every 10 minutes around the clock and pages your on-call team when it stops answering or starts stating errors.

## Before you start

- PagerDuty or Better Stack is connected, so the alert reaches whoever is on call. See [Alerts](/monitor/alerts#connect-a-channel).
- If your agent only makes outbound calls, it can start a call when Bluejay asks. See [Outbound agents](/monitor/uptime/overview#outbound-agents).

## Steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create an uptime monitor">
    In **Observability → Uptime**, click **Create** and select your agent under **Agent**. Set **Frequency** to **Every 10 minutes** and leave **Runs on** at **Always**. A monitor has one schedule, made of its days and one range of hours, so running it all the time is the only way to cover both nights and weekends.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep the health checks on">
    Keep **Agent Malfunction** and **Agent Responds** on, so an agent that states an error or stops answering counts as a failure. See [Checks](/monitor/uptime/overview#checks).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send failures to your on-call tool">
    Click **Next**, then under **Notifications** turn on **PagerDuty Notifications** or **Better Stack Notifications**, and click **Create**. Your escalation policy in that tool decides who gets paged. See [Incidents](/monitor/alerts#incidents). The first check starts right away and shows under **Health check logs** on the monitor.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Check the result

Each run under **Health check logs** shows one of these verdicts:

- **Up** means all checks passed.
- **Down** means at least one check failed. Bluejay opens an incident in PagerDuty or Better Stack, and resolves it when the monitor recovers.
- **Unknown** means Bluejay could not measure the run, for example because of an error on its side. It does not count as your agent being down.

If the first run is **Down**, open it and read the transcript to see which check failed before you rely on the monitor overnight.
