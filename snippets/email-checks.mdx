Mail servers run these checks to decide whether an email really comes from the domain it claims, and whether it goes to the inbox or to spam. A failed check is a common reason a reply never reaches the customer.

- **SPF** (Sender Policy Framework) checks whether the server that sent the email is allowed to send for the sender's domain. The domain lists its allowed servers in its DNS, the public records that describe a domain.
- **DKIM** (DomainKeys Identified Mail) checks the email's digital signature. The sending domain signs each email with a private key and publishes the matching public key in its DNS. A valid signature proves the domain sent the email and nobody changed it on the way. An unsigned email fails.
- **DMARC** (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance) checks that the domain in the **From** address is the same domain that passed SPF or DKIM. This match is called alignment. The domain must also publish a DMARC policy, a DNS record that tells receivers what to do with mail that fails. A domain with no DMARC policy fails, even when SPF and DKIM pass.
- **Spam** is a spam score that a receiving mail server added to the email. Lower is better. A score of about 5 or more usually means spam.
- **N/A** means there was nothing to judge, for example because the email carries no verdict from a receiving server.
