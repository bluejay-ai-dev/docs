A personality is the same Digital Human delivered a different way. The scenario, the intent and the success criteria never change — only how the caller sounds and behaves. One Digital Human with three personalities runs four conversations per simulation: the base plus one for each personality.

## What You'll Learn

- What a personality changes and what it leaves alone
- How to add personalities to one or many Digital Humans
- How personalities show up in a simulation run

## Why Personalities Exist

Most teams end up with copies of the same test case: "Refund request", "Refund request (Spanish)", "Refund request (noisy)". The copies drift. Someone fixes the success criteria on one and forgets the other two, and the run stops comparing like with like.

A personality keeps one test case and varies only the delivery, so a failure tells you something real: the agent handles this scenario in English but not in Spanish, or handles it on a clean line but not over traffic noise.

<Note>
A personality can never change what the test is. Intent, success criteria, scripted responses and tool permissions all stay on the Digital Human.
</Note>

## What a Personality Owns

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Voice" icon="microphone">
    Language, accent and gender. A personality that speaks Spanish carries the whole voice, so the Digital Human still sounds like a real caller in that language.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Background noise" icon="waveform">
    The ambient sound and its volume — traffic, a cafe, a call centre.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audio quality and fluency" icon="signal">
    Line quality and how fluently the caller speaks the language.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Pacing" icon="gauge">
    Speaking speed, verbosity, patience before the caller speaks again, and silence timeout.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Interruptions" icon="scissors">
    Whether the caller cuts the agent off, and how often.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Creativity" icon="shuffle">
    How much the caller improvises around the scenario.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Adding Personalities

Open a Digital Human and go to the **Behavior** tab. The row of chips at the top is the personality selector: **Base** is the Digital Human itself, and every chip after it is a personality. Pick a chip and the Behavior fields below show that personality's values — edit them the same way you edit the Digital Human.

The **+** button opens the create dialog. It offers five presets, and you can build your own:

| Preset | What it changes |
| --- | --- |
| Spanish | The voice, matching the base caller's gender |
| Background noise | Traffic at 0.8 volume |
| Interruptions | Frequent interruptions and less patience |
| Poor audio | Low line quality, intermediate fluency |
| Fast talker | Fast speech, high verbosity |

### Several Digital Humans at Once

Select Digital Humans in the list and click **Create Personalities** to write the same set to all of them. Each Digital Human gets its own copy, so you can edit them independently afterwards. Presets adapt per Digital Human — Spanish keeps each caller's own gender — unless you edit the fields in the dialog, which pins those values for every selected Digital Human.

## Personalities in a Simulation Run

A Digital Human with personalities produces one conversation per personality plus the base run. On the run results page those conversations are grouped under the Digital Human, with a badge showing how many personalities ran. Expand the row to see each one.

Open a conversation and the **Digital Human** tab in the side panel shows the personality that conversation actually ran as — only that one, with no way to switch. What you are reading is what the caller did.

<Tip>
Every conversation is evaluated on its own, against the same success criteria. A pass rate of 75% across the four conversations means three deliveries worked and one did not — and the run tells you which.
</Tip>

## Limits

- A [customer journey](/test/simulations/customer-journeys) keeps its own step count and does not fan out into personalities.
- Personalities are rows on a saved Digital Human, so they cannot be added to unsaved drafts.
- Changing a personality does not change conversations that already ran.
