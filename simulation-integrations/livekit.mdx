There are three ways to run Bluejay simulations against a LiveKit agent. Which one you need depends on two things: how your agent is deployed, and who starts the call.

- **Inbound agent**: your agent answers calls. Bluejay's Digital Human starts the conversation.
- **Outbound agent**: your agent places calls. Bluejay's Digital Human answers.

| You want to test... | Use this guide |
|---|---|
| An inbound agent on the LiveKit Agents framework | [Direct connection](/simulation-integrations/livekit-direct) (recommended) |
| An inbound agent behind SIP infrastructure | [SIP connection](/simulation-integrations/livekit-sip) |
| An outbound agent (it dials customers) | [Outbound agents](/simulation-integrations/livekit-outbound) |

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Direct — inbound" icon="bolt" href="/simulation-integrations/livekit-direct">
    Bluejay creates a room in your LiveKit project, dispatches your agent into it, and joins the Digital Human. No SIP, no trunks.
  </Card>
  <Card title="SIP — inbound" icon="phone-volume" href="/simulation-integrations/livekit-sip">
    Bluejay dials your project's SIP host. You provision an inbound trunk and dispatch rule.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Outbound" icon="phone-arrow-up-right" href="/simulation-integrations/livekit-outbound">
    Bluejay gives each Digital Human a number to answer on and notifies your system. Your agent dials.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  Testing an inbound agent with no specific reason to use SIP? Use **Direct connection**. It's fewer moving parts and avoids trunk and dispatch configuration.
</Note>
